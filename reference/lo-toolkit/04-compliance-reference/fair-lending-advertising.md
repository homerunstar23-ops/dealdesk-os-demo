**ECOA / Regulation B** prohibits discrimination in any aspect of a credit transaction (including business credit) on prohibited bases, and Reg B §1002.4(b) prohibits oral or written statements in advertising or otherwise that would discourage, on a prohibited basis, a reasonable person from applying.
- Reg B: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/ · §1002.4: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/4/

**Fair Housing Act** prohibits discrimination in residential real estate-related transactions, including making, printing or publishing discriminatory advertisements. HUD overview: https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp/fair_housing_act_overview · DOJ: https://www.justice.gov/crt/fair-housing-act-1

**UDAAP** — unfair, deceptive or abusive acts or practices. CFPB examination procedures: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/supervision-examinations/unfair-deceptive-or-abusive-acts-or-practices-udaaps-examination-procedures/ · CFPB fair lending: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/fair-lending/

**Advertising pitfalls to avoid**
- Targeting or excluding audiences (ad platforms, mailing lists, ZIP codes) in ways that correlate with protected characteristics — "redlining" risk; document legitimate business criteria
- Imagery/wording suggesting a preferred type of borrower (age, family status, national origin, religion, sex, disability)
- "Guaranteed approval," "everyone qualifies," "no credit check," "lowest rate," or undisclosed fees — deception risk
- Teaser rates/payments without required disclosures and sponsor approval (Reg Z §1026.24 for consumer credit ads)
- Fake urgency, fabricated testimonials, invented statistics, implying government affiliation
- Inconsistent treatment — ask every prospect the same intake questions; apply the same follow-up cadence
